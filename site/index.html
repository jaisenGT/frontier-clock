How long does the frontier stay ahead?

Many people think Chinese and open-source models are catching up to the US-led frontier. The size of the gap influences whether the US can afford an AI pause and the practicality of US-China coordination on AI safety. Frontier Clock uses Epoch AI’s benchmark data (CC BY 4.0) to track these trends and others.

Method

  1. Capability. For the selected benchmarks we fit an item-response model following the method of Epoch AI's Capabilities Index (ECI): score ≈ sigmoid(ab·(Cm − Db)), with a capability C per model and a difficulty D and slope a per benchmark. This puts models evaluated on different benchmarks on one scale.
  2. ECI-scaled capability. Capabilities are shown on the ECI scale. With all benchmarks we pin the same two reference models Epoch uses (Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 = 150); our scores then match Epoch's published ECI with r ≈ 0.997, typically within about a point. For a subset of benchmarks we rescale to match the all-benchmarks scores of the same models, so every selection uses the same points. These are our own fits, not Epoch's official ECI values, and will differ from them for domain subsets.
  3. Frontier. A model is a frontier record if, at release, it was more capable than every model released before it. The frontier function F(t) is built from those records, either as a running max (a staircase) or by interpolating between records.
  4. Frontier-equivalent date. A model's score is F−1(C): the date the frontier first reached its capability.
  5. Gap. At each month, we compare the best model available from each group with the best available overall.
  6. 90% intervals. Each resample makes as many random draws as there are selected benchmarks (currently 47 for all benchmarks), with replacement, so a benchmark can count twice or drop out; any one benchmark is left out about a third of the time. The same draws apply to every model. We refit everything on each resample, repeat, and take the 5th–95th percentiles. Benchmark scores themselves are fixed; the intervals show how much the results depend on which benchmarks are included. All benchmarks and each single domain use 1,000 precomputed resamples; any other selection runs 100 resamples in your browser, so its interval ends are less precise (about ±0.3 months). With all benchmarks, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-5 are pinned to 130 and 150 in every refit, so they have no capability interval and models near them have narrow ones. For a benchmark subset, each refit is rescaled to the all-benchmarks scores, which removes uncertainty about the scale itself, so subset capability intervals look narrower than they are. Dates don't depend on the scale and aren't affected.
  7. Coverage.

Caveats

  • Dates are of public release; labs often hold stronger models internally.
  • If frontier progress accelerates, a constant capability gap shows up as a shrinking gap in months. Switch the gap chart to capability units to check.
  • Scores come from Epoch's internal runs and external reports and inherit their elicitation, contamination and harness differences.
  • The gap lines for China and open weights start only once a group's best model passes GPT-4 (early 2025 and mid-2024). Before that they were more than a GPT-4 generation behind, which this data can't date.
  • Domain assignments are ours and editable in data/domains.json.

Related work

Frontier Clock was inspired by Epoch AI's finding that Chinese AI models have lagged the US frontier by 7 months on average and by Parv Mahajan's “The US-China AI gap is very uncertain” (MCNAIR). Both measure how far Chinese models trail the US frontier; Frontier Clock extends the idea to non-China open-weights models, individual labs and benchmark domains.

Data and attribution

All benchmark scores come from Epoch AI, “Capabilities & benchmarking”, used under CC BY 4.0. Frontier Clock changes this data: it refits capabilities with its own model and benchmark selection, leaves out models weaker than GPT-4 (Mar 2023) and benchmarks that only scored them, fills in missing metadata, and assigns benchmarks to domains.
